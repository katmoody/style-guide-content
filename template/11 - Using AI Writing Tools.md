# 11 - Using AI writing tools

AI tools can help you draft, edit, and summarize faster. They can also make things up, sound confident about it, and leak information you didn't mean to share. These rules keep the speed and lose the risk.

## The rules

- **You own what you publish.** It doesn't matter who or what wrote the first draft.
- **Never paste sensitive information into an AI tool that isn't approved** (see §10). That includes customer data, credentials, and anything confidential.
- **Check every fact, link, name, and step.** AI tools can invent details that sound right but aren't. Test steps yourself when you can.
- **Make it sound like us.** Edit AI drafts to match this guide's voice and rules (see §06).

## Good uses

- Drafting an outline or first draft
- Tightening wordy writing
- Summarizing long threads or meeting notes
- Checking a draft against this guide

**Tip:** Give the AI tool our Quick Rules (§01) along with your draft, and ask it to flag anything that doesn't follow them.

## Not good uses

- The final word on facts, policies, or legal questions
- Writing about anything you can't check yourself
- Anything involving sensitive information, unless the tool is approved for it
